Overview

I built mCall in grades 9–10, during the COVID-19 pandemic, when video calls and online classes had become part of everyday life. It was my attempt at building a browser-based video conferencing application, with a slightly different approach to how participants connected to one another.

Rather than automatically connecting everyone in a meeting, mCall allowed participants to choose which peers they wanted to connect to, and to refuse individual connections. It used peer-to-peer WebRTC connections with encrypted media streams, and supported screen sharing, mobile and desktop browsers, and classroom features such as raising your hand.

It's one of my earlier projects, and I haven't worked on it in years. I've kept the source code and a demonstration here as a record of what I built at the time.

The source code is available [a href="https://github.com/adrianvla/mCall"]on GitHub[/a].

Features

  • Peer-to-peer video calls
  • Encrypted WebRTC media streams
  • Individual control over peer connections
  • Screen sharing
  • Mobile and desktop browser support
  • Online classroom features, including raising your hand
  • Open-source
